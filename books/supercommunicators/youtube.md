You come home wiped out and start venting about your day — and your partner immediately starts handing you solutions. A minute later you're both hurt and alienated. Why does that keep happening?

In Supercommunicators, Charles Duhigg argues that connection has almost nothing to do with being articulate, charming, or extroverted. Our worst misunderstandings happen when two people are confidently having DIFFERENT KINDS of conversations at the same time — and a "supercommunicator" is simply the person working hardest to figure out which conversation you're really in, then matching it.

This deep dive breaks down the science and the stories: the three kinds of every conversation (practical, emotional, social), the Matching Principle, the CIA officer who recruited a spy only after he got vulnerable, Arthur Aron's 36 questions that make strangers fall in love, "looping for understanding," and the Dartmouth research showing that when we truly connect, our brains literally sync. Plus the hardest question the book raises head-on: is any of this just manipulation?

One question ties it all together: What's this really about?
